## Throughlines

- Postel teaches restitutio omnium: one linguistic family, one Kabbalah read as Christian, one feminine messiah, one world monarchy.
- Esoteric commentators, citing Postel's Clavis and Lévi, treat the 56 Minor Arcana as a machine of Yod-He-Vau-He across three worlds, with suits aligned to crosier, chalice, cross, and host.
- Rosicrucianism in the briefing has three epochs, then splits by 1618 into a magical reading and a Pietist reading.
- Bureus turns Postel's linguistics, Dee's Monas, and the rune Bjarkan into the Adulruna, and names Gustav II Adolf the Lion of the North.
- The closing table is the synthesis: five systems, each with a text, a mechanism, and an aim.

## Figures

- Guillaume Postel, 1510–1581. Linguist, Christian Kabbalist, prophetic universalist.
- Mère Jeanne, the Venetian Virgin, encountered in 1547. Postel calls her mater mundi and the New Eve.
- Abraham von Franckenberg, editor of the Clavis in 1646. Friend and biographer of Jacob Böhme.
- Éliphas Lévi and Papus, the commentators the briefing cites for the Tarot structure. The briefing does not say Postel designed the cards.
- Christian Rosencreutz, the knight of the Fama. Johann Valentin Andreae, author of the Chymische Hochzeit.
- Philippus a Gabella, Michael Maier, Robert Fludd on the magical side. Andreae and Melchior Breler on the Pietist side.
- Johannes Bureus. Prose and the closing table: 1568–1652. Royal archivist and tutor to Gustav II Adolf.
- John Dee, Monas Hieroglyphica, 1564. Paracelsus, Helisaeus Roeslin, and Paul Grebner behind the northern lion.

## Dates and epochs

- Postel: Arabic grammar and twelve alphabets, 1538. Venice and the Clavis, 1547. Zohar and Sefer Yetzirah in Latin, 1552. Inquisition, 1555–1559. Confinement from 1563.
- Franckenberg’s Clavis, Amsterdam, 1646, during the Thirty Years' War.
- Rosicrucian epochs: 1610–1620, 1620–1660, 1710–1740. Manifestos 1614, 1615, 1616. The split is dated 1618.
- Confessio, Frankfurt 1615, corrects Damcar to Damar.
- Bureus receives Dee’s Monas from the Posen library in 1610. Vision at Stora Tuna, 5 December 1613, 6:30 in the morning. Manuals 1611 and 1643. Pamphlets 1616 and 1622.
- Lion applied to the Swedish crown and the Protestant League, 1630–1632. Erfurt coin, 1633.
- Chronograms as Bureus reads them: 1673 from the Virgil capitals, 1666 from the crucified heart.

## Terms

- Restitutio omnium: restitution of all things, Postel’s universal reconciliation.
- Immutation: Postel’s claim that Mère Jeanne’s spirit passed into his body. Pseudonym Elias Pandocheus, Elias the All-Receiver.
- Yod-He-Vau-He: the Tetragrammaton the commentators use to govern court cards, number series, and the four suits.
- Adulruna: Dee’s Monas set on the rune Bjarkan. A crucified sun inside an infinite circle.
- Damcar / Damar: the Fama’s secret city, corrected in 1615 to Damar in Yemen. Esoteric gloss in the briefing: blood of the Lamb.
- Leo Septentrionalis: the Lion of the North, der Löwe aus der Mitternacht.

## Relations

- Postel’s Clavis is the book Franckenberg reopens in 1646, and one of the two books the Tarot commentators cite.
- The second He, the Pentacles, is the passage from the 56 Minor Arcana to the 22 Major Arcana.
- The three worlds share the numbers 4 and 7. The Knave and the ten are the transition term.
- Bureus uses Postel’s De Originibus for a Japhetic line (Noah, Japheth, Gomer, Askenaz) and Dee’s glyph for the Adulruna.
- Thor, Othin, and Freya are father, son, and holy spirit in Bureus’s Uppsala triad.
- The lion prophecy is Paracelsian first, then applied to Gustav II Adolf.

## Quotes

- "Close up the streams, young boy, for the meadows have drunk themselves full." Virgil, Eclogues III, as the briefing says Bureus heard it at Stora Tuna.
- "Writing with Nature and Nature with Writing." Franckenberg’s phrase for Scripture and Nature joined in Christ. Not given its own slide.
- "that no author could disclose." Bureus’s claim about Revelations 5:5. Not given its own slide.
- Latin chronogram lines are in the briefing and in the speaker notes of the vision slide. They are not a second quotation slide.

## Caveats

- The Tarot laws are the briefing’s report of commentators who reference Postel and Lévi, not a claim that Postel invented the pack.
- Prophecy, immutation, the invisible order, and the Lion of the North are claims of those figures, reported as such.
- A section diagram heads Bureus with a death year before his birth. The prose and the closing table say 1568–1652. The deck uses 1568–1652 and does not repeat the impossible year.
- The briefing’s parenthetical dates for Helisaeus Roeslin put his death before his birth, and also have him writing in 1578. The impossible span is omitted. He stays as the author of Theoria Nova Meteoron (1578) and an expander of the northern prophecy. No replacement death year is guessed.
- The twelve alphabets, the imprisonment narrative, the initiate chain, the Sabean and Ssabian temple lore, and the pamphlet list are kept in the source and in speaker notes. They do not each receive a slide.
